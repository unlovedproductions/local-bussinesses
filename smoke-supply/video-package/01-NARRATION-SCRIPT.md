### Continuous VO

Here's an example of how online ordering, your POS, and inventory could work together.

A customer visits the Smoke & Supply website, finds a product, and places an order for in-store pickup.

That order can then move from the website into the store's connected systems, so it doesn't have to remain separate from the rest of the store's sales.

The order can appear for store staff, showing what was ordered and that it's ready to be prepared for pickup.

Now let's follow that same product. We start with 25 units. After the online purchase, the connected inventory could reflect 24.

Later, two more units are sold at the counter. The POS records the sale, and the inventory could change from 24 to 22.

That updated quantity can then be synchronized back to the website, so the online availability reflects the connected inventory.

As that inventory continues to fall, the system can flag a product when it reaches the store's minimum stock level.

For example, this product has eight units available, a minimum of ten, and average daily sales of four. Based on the store's own rules and supplier information, the system could suggest a reorder quantity.

The goal is to connect the pieces: online orders, in-store sales, and inventory information.

That gives the owner a clearer view of what's selling, what's running low, and what may need attention—without treating each part as a completely separate process.

This is a simulated concept. The actual setup would depend on the store's POS, inventory system, and available integrations.
